### Added

- **`femg_refit2026` weights each value by its published D error and gives each experiment its own offset (2026-10-15).** Each value's variance in the fit is now its published D error (per value for Chakraborty 1997, Holzapfel et al. 2007 and Spandler and O'Neill 2010; the study's stated error for Dohmen et al. 2007 and Petry et al. 2004) plus its input errors, a common extra scatter, and random offsets per laboratory and per experiment. The per-experiment offset counts each of Chakraborty's 12 diffusion couples once for temperature, though each gives 3-9 values. D[001] at the KS20 round-robin conditions is +0.177 ± 0.132 log against Dohmen and Chakraborty (2007) (was +0.224 ± 0.102); E 209 ± 9 kJ/mol (was ± 6.8); composition coefficient 2.82 (was 3.15); spread between laboratories 0.072 log. Fe-Mg times are about 1.11x longer than with the version before. A bootstrap of experiments is now within the fitted uncertainty for every quantity (E 8.6 against 9.3 kJ/mol). The law-uncertainty Monte Carlo check now also draws the activation volume. The preset's description text is rewritten for readers new to the refit.
- **Monte Carlo over temperature, pressure, fO2 and the law: `forster.rescale` (2026-10-15).** `monte_carlo(result, ConditionUncertainty(T_sd_C, P_sd_Pa, fO2_sd_log, fO2_buffer, law, between_study), posterior=None, n, seed)` draws the conditions (fO2 optionally held relative to QFM or NNO, so it follows each drawn T and P) and the law's parameters from its covariance (plus optionally the spread between laboratories), and the fit's time from its own posterior or from an initial-condition posterior (`forster.sampling`). A fixed-rim fit is rescaled, t = t_fit D_fit / D_draw (checked against refits at shifted conditions to within the time step); fits with growth stages or a changing rim are refitted per draw. Returns the draws, the 5/16/50/84/95th percentiles and the sd of log10 t from each source alone. Law only, its added spread equals the fit's linear law sigma within 5% (`tests/test_rescale.py`). Seeded, unlike the paper-era script. Tables: `mc_T_sd_C`, `mc_P_sd_Pa`, `mc_fO2_sd_log`, `mc_fO2_buffer` (default: the row's own buffer), `mc_law`, `mc_between_study`, `mc_samples`, `mc_seed`; summary.csv gains `mc_method`, `mc_draws`, `mc_median_days`, `mc_low_days`, `mc_high_days`, `mc_p05_days`, `mc_p95_days`, `mc_sd_log10`; `forster fit` writes `<run>_mc.csv` and `<run>_time.png` (`forster.plot_time_distributions`: the fit alone, with the initial condition, with conditions and law).
- **Law-comparison figure (2026-10-15).** `forster.plot_law_comparison(results)` draws one profile's best fits under several laws (they usually coincide: a profile fixes D t, so each law reaches the same curve at its own time) and `forster.plot_law_times(results)` the best-fit time under each law with its 1-sigma range from the profile and, where the law has a covariance, the wider range with the law's calibration, on a log time axis. `forster fit --compare-laws` writes both as `<run>_laws.png`.
- **Fixed rim value and growth stages in parameter tables (2026-10-15).** `rim_fo` (Fo held at the rim; blank = edge_fo) and the list columns `stage_days`, `stage_rim_um`, `stage_zone_fo`, `stage_rim_fo` (one entry per `Stage`, written like `steps_um`) reach `fit_profile` from tables and `forster fit`; summary.csv writes them back so it refits identically, and gains `time_since_last_stage_days`. Staged runs need `dx_um` and `dt_s` given (the grid choice does not model stages yet); the initial-condition scan refuses runs with stages or `rim_fo`, and a rim that changes in time (`RimPath`) stays Python-only.
- **Initial-condition fitting by optimisation and sampling: `forster.sampling` (2026-10-15).** `fit_initial_condition(profile, conditions, orientation, initial, uncertainties, grid, ...)` finds the best-fit initial condition (Nelder-Mead on the uncertain step positions and Fo values, time marginalised: one model run per evaluation gives every time step) and the posterior of time over those parameters: importance sampling from a Laplace proposal (multivariate t from a finite-difference Hessian, 1024 scrambled-Sobol draws, seeded), or dynesty nested sampling when the effective sample size is below a quarter of the draws (a parameter flat across a wide prior). It returns the best values and time, the central 68% and median of the marginal posterior of time, each parameter's posterior mean and sd, its sensitivity dE[t]/dvalue, the share of the variance of time from the initial condition (law of total variance), and the evidence. On the same likelihood, Laplace and nested sampling agree with a dense grid to within 1-3% (0.3% for 4096 draws); on round robin profiles the Laplace path takes about 1,000 model runs and under a second, the nested fallback up to about 15,000 runs (`analyses/ic_sampling/`). In parameter tables, `scan_method` (auto, the default; laplace; nested; grid) and `scan_seed`; summary.csv gains `scan_sampler`, `scan_median_days`, `scan_ic_share`, `scan_sensitivity`, `scan_ess`, `scan_log_evidence`, and `<run>_scan.csv` holds the posterior of time. A table that gives `scan_points` without `scan_method` keeps the grid scan, so earlier tables and summaries refit as before. New dependency: dynesty.

- DIPRA's finite-difference scheme (Girona and Costa 2013, eq. 5) as an alternative to the engine's: `fit_profile(..., scheme="dipra")`, `RunDefaults(scheme="dipra")`, or a `scheme` column in a parameter table. Both schemes solve the same equation and differ only in how the cross term is differenced (DIPRA: forward differences and the analytic dD/dX_Fo; engine: central differences of C and D), so fitting a profile with both is a check on the discretization. - **Initial-condition uncertainty: `forster.scan` (2026-10-13).** `scan_initial_conditions` fits a set of candidate initial conditions (for example `step_offsets(initial, range(-6, 7))`, the step at -6..+6 um) and gives the best candidate and time, a profile-likelihood 1-sigma range of time (lower envelope of the candidates' chi-squared) and a marginal one (noise scale integrated out), plus each candidate's probability. One run per candidate; time is never sampled. `progress(i, n)` callback; warns if the best candidate is at the end of the scan. On synthetic profiles with the step 3 um off, coverage of the true time rises from 21-23% (single fit) to 65-74% and the bias from +18% to about +1%. On round robin profile KS20-527_2-transect-1 the scan moves the step from 41 to 46 um (the nominal step is rejected by delta chi2 = 14) and the time from 42 to 24 days.
- **Uncertainties on each step and concentration (2026-10-13).** `forster.scan.Uncertainty(spread, kind)` per parameter (`step1_um`, `step2_um`, `edge_fo`, `core_fo`, `step_fo`): Gaussian (sigma; grid over +-2.5 sigma, prior weight and a ((v - v0)/sigma)^2 penalty in the profile range) or uniform (+- half-width; flat). `candidate_grid` builds the grid (13, 9 or 5 points per parameter by default; invalid nodes dropped and counted; over 2000 candidates refused). In `parameters.csv`: `steps_sd_um` / `steps_range_um` (one value or one per step), `edge_fo_sd` / `_range`, `core_fo_sd` / `_range`, `step_fo_sd` / `_range`, `scan_points`. `forster fit` scans those runs, prints the number of candidates first, writes `<run>_scan.csv`, and adds `scan_*` columns to summary.csv (which refits the same scan); `--max-candidates`. Python: `forster.scan_run`, `scan_summary`, `scan_table`. Scans run on one grid with dt raised until D dt / dx^2 = 0.4 (`scan_grid`; dx unchanged): 2.3-5.8x faster, best fit within 0.2%; coarsening dx instead biased it by up to 12%. With uniform uncertainties containing the truth, a two-parameter scan (step and edge both off) covered it 69% (profile) and 67% (marginal) of the time.
- `forster.uncertainty.posterior_interval`: the Bayesian 1-sigma range with the noise scale integrated out (flat prior on time, 1/s on s). Agrees with the default range to within 5 points of coverage; on real profiles it is about as wide and slightly longer (`docs/references/likelihood-method.md`, section 6).
- **Diffusivity-law uncertainty in fitted times (2026-10-13).** `forster.LawUncertainty` (a covariance over named law parameters, `forster.diffusivity.LAW_PARAMETERS`, plus the spread between laboratories) on `ArrheniusDiffusivity.uncertainty`; `ArrheniusDiffusivity.log10_sigma(conditions, orientation, x_fo)` gives the 1-sigma of log10 D (checked against finite differences of the law over all eight parameters). New preset `femg_refit2026`: the refit of the TaMED Fe-Mg data in the Dohmen and Chakraborty (2007) form with a random offset per study (`analyses/femg_refit`, written by `export_preset.py`, which checks it reproduces the refit to 1e-14), D +0.27 log above the default at the round robin KS20 conditions, 1-sigma 0.12 log, between-study spread 0.25 log. Because an Fo profile depends on D t, the law's error maps onto log10 time exactly, with no extra model runs: `FitResult.law_sigma_log10`, `between_study_log10` and `time_range_days("fit" | "law" | "law_and_studies")` (`forster.uncertainty.widen_log10` combines each side in log10); summary.csv gains `law_sigma_log10`, `between_study_log10`, `law_low_days`, `law_high_days`, `all_low_days`, `all_high_days` (empty for laws without a covariance, so default results are unchanged). The composition term is evaluated a third of the way from edge to core Fo (measured: within 0.0011 log of the fitted time shift; it moves sigma by under 0.001). Not for growth stages, rim values that change in time or element fits yet (`docs/references/law-uncertainty-limits.md`). Plan: `docs/plans/2026-10-13-law-uncertainty.md`.

- **Compiled time steppers (2026-10-13), optional.** With Numba installed (`forster[fast]` extra; in the dev group) the central, dipra and implicit steppers run as compiled loops (`forster.compiled`, `forster._kernels`) when the diffusivity is log-linear in Fo (every built-in law; `forster.diffusivity.LogLinearDiffusivity` carries its parameters). - **Implicit scheme with the boundary on a node (2026-10-13).** `scheme = "implicit"` (`forster.implicit`): the rim and core values are held on the end nodes (the explicit schemes hold them one node outside), the start is the step function averaged over each node's cell instead of snapped to nodes, and time steps are TR-BDF2 (L-stable, second order, one-step). The fitted time becomes second order in dx: with the chosen grid (dx = l / 8 final, l / 4 scans; 400 or 80 time steps up to the fit) it was within 0.3% of a converged reference on all 35 real profiles, and a fit takes a median 0.13 s instead of 0.9 s. Both changes are needed: either alone left errors up to 12-22% at l / dx = 8. Legacy uncertainty and element fits do not support it yet.

  - Growth stages with the implicit scheme start at their exact times and the grid has a node on every stage rim; a staged run matches an explicit on-node solver to 1e-4 Fo.
  - `advance(c, n, first_row=...)` (internal) carries the time; `model.scheme_grid` builds either kind of grid; `choose_grid` / `check_grid` take `scheme` (implicit: no stability limit; dt checked against time resolution); `grid_stability` is empty for implicit fits.
  - Kriging models are cached per profile (`engine._kriging_model`; identical results): building one was 89% of an implicit fit's time.
  - **Correction to the grid choice below:** against the converged reference the explicit `final` grid is off by a median 1.1% and up to 3.6% (18 of 35 profiles above 1%), not within 1%; its calibration used an explicit reference with the same first-order bias. Corrected: see Changed. Details: `docs/references/grid-choice.md`.
- **Automatic grid choice (2026-10-13).** Leave `dx_um` and `dt_s` blank and `forster` picks them: dx so the fitted time is within 1% (`final`) or 5% (scans: `scan_dx_um`, `scan_dt_s`, `scan_max_time_days`, all optional), from the diffusion length of a quick coarse fit (error of the time about 0.65 dx / l, measured on six real profiles), and never above a quarter of the narrowest gap between steps; dt at D_max dt / dx^2 = 0.4 for the fastest species; the longest time 3x the coarse fit. Give either part and the other is chosen. Measurements: `docs/references/grid-choice.md`, `docs/plans/2026-10-13-grid-choice/`. The existing parameter tables' grids are stable (ratio 0.007-0.17) and unchanged.

- **Ni, Mn and Ca diffusion alongside Fo (2026-10-13), Python.** `forster.elements`: Ni and Mn are modelled with the Fo profile diffusing alongside them (their diffusivity depends on X_Fo), Ca without it (its DIPRA diffusivity does not). `fit_element(profile, conditions, orientation, fo_initial, initial, grid)` fits one element for its own time and 1-sigma range (the correlated method of `fit_profile`, with its early stopping); `fit_joint(fo_profile, element_profiles, ...)` fits one time to Fo and elements together and reports each species' own best time, its chi-squared rise at the joint best fit and `consistency_p` (do they share one time; warns below 0.05). `ElementProfile` (any unit: wt% oxide, ppm), `ElementInitial` (steps default to Fo's), `FittedInitial` (the default: edge, core and plateau concentrations fitted by least squares at every time step, which is exact because the element equation is linear in them for a given Fo history). On KS20-527_2-transect-1, Ni (15.5 d) disagrees with Fo (42 d, p = 0.008) and Ca agrees (p = 0.53). Explicit schemes only; tables and CLI to follow. - **Instantaneous growth stages (2026-10-13), Python.** `fit_profile(..., stages=[forster.Stage(start_s, rim_um, rim_value, zone_value), ...])`: at each stage's start the rim moves outward and the new zone appears, then diffuses with the older crystal (or only the rim value changes). A staged run matches an independent solver with the same history (3.4e-4 explicit; 1e-4 implicit). A staged synthetic profile (6 d, then 4 d after an overgrowth) fitted with the single-stage model gives 6.8 d (6.2-7.6), containing neither true time, with as good a chi-squared: the fit cannot tell the histories apart. ### Changed

- **Explicit schemes substep within dt; dt sets the time resolution for every scheme (2026-10-15).** A chosen final grid for `central` or `dipra` used to keep every stable step: for the packaged example (dx 0.045 um at l / dx = 240) that was 426,385 rows of 1,530 nodes, 5.2 GB in memory and 13 s, almost all of it spent storing and scoring rows 2.9 s apart for a 5-day fit. Now `choose_grid` gives the explicit schemes the same dt as the implicit one (1/400 of the fitted time for a final fit, 1/80 for a scan) or the stable step if that is longer, and `fit_profile` and the scans take ceil(ratio / 0.4) stable steps per kept row when D dt / dx^2 exceeds 0.5 (`model._explicit_substeps`; compiled and NumPy steppers). The example: 18 MB and 6.0 s, fitted time 5.137 d against 5.14 d (within the 0.25% resolution); a substepped run equals the stable run at every kept row to 1e-13 (`tests/test_grid.py`). A dt at or below the stability limit runs exactly as before, so legacy and given grids are unchanged (the differential tests pass). Unstable explicit grids are no longer refused, except with growth stages, whose start times need a stable dt; `check_grid` no longer raises for them. `forster.grid.implicit_dt` is now `resolving_dt`.
- **Kriging only for the legacy method (2026-10-13).** The default (correlated) method fits the measured points, so `fit_profile` no longer krigs the profile for it: `FitResult.interp_*` are empty and `forster fit` writes `<run>_kriged.csv` only for legacy runs. Fits are unchanged; kriging (pykrige's leave-one-out statistics, up to 0.45 s per profile) had been computed on every call, including the coarse fits that choose the grid.

- **The implicit scheme is the default (2026-10-13).** `fit_profile`, `RunDefaults`, parameter tables without a `scheme` column, scans and `choose_grid` now use `implicit` (`forster.model.DEFAULT_SCHEME`); `central` is still the default with `uncertainty = "legacy"`, so legacy results reproduce exactly, and element fits keep `central`. Refitting the existing round robin and paper tables (their own grids, dx 1 and 0.5 um) moves fitted times by a median 2.7% and up to 8.1%, 28 of 35 shorter: on those grids `central` was off by a median 2.6% (up to 8.7%) against a converged reference and `implicit` by 0.05% (up to 0.5%). Those tables' small time steps (60-600 s, set for the explicit stability limit) make implicit fits slower there (`check_grid` warns that dt is smaller than needed); leave `dx_um` and `dt_s` empty to have them chosen. The template's worked example now leaves `dx_um`, `dt_s` and `scheme` empty.
  - The explicit grid rule is corrected: error about 2.4 dx / l (was 0.65, calibrated against a biased explicit reference), so `choose_grid(scheme="central")` gives l / dx = 240 (final) and 48 (scans), and `grid_error_pct` and the coarse-grid warnings for explicit fits are about 3.7x larger than before.

- `FitResult` gains `element` ("Fe-Mg" unless an element fit) and `last_stage_s` (0 without growth stages); `plot_fit` draws element fits in their own units and greys measured points outside the model domain.
- **Small-sample correction to the 1-sigma range (2026-10-13), default.** The range is where chi-squared rises by n/(n - p) x t^2(0.8413; n - p) instead of 1 (`forster.uncertainty.interval_threshold`): 1.48 for 6 points, 1.07 for 29, 1.007 for 300. It corrects for the noise being estimated from the same points. Ranges widen by up to 22% for 6 points, 4% for 29, nothing for dense profiles; on synthetic 6-point profiles coverage rises from 49-57% to 60-66% (target 68%). Legacy is unchanged.
- **Early stopping (2026-10-13), default for the correlated method.** The model runs in blocks and stops once it is 2x past the best fit and the residuals are 4x their minimum and rising, extending if the 1-sigma range reaches the end. Fits are identical to full runs on all 35 real profiles; fitting them takes 19 s instead of 129 s. `FitResult.curves` now ends a margin past the fit rather than at `max_time_days`. Legacy always runs the full time.
- **Noise-model warning only for a profile-wide misfit (2026-10-13).** It used to fire whenever the correlation length or amplitude sat at either end of its search grid (25-35% of fits to pure white noise, with no effect on coverage). It now fires when the misfit spans the profile (L at half its length) with an amplitude of at least half the white noise; those cases covered 50-55% against 62-67% otherwise. False alarms: about 1% on pure white noise at 29 points, 8-22% on 6- and 10-point profiles.

- **New default for the uncertainty on the fitted time (2026-10-12).** The best fit and its 1-sigma range now come from the measured points rather than the kriged profile: each model curve is interpolated to the analyses, their errors are modelled as white noise plus a smooth correlated misfit (fitted by maximum likelihood, `FitResult.noise`), and the range is where that chi-squared rises by 1, interpolated between time steps (`forster/uncertainty.py`). Select with `fit_profile(..., uncertainty=...)`, `RunDefaults(uncertainty=...)` or an `uncertainty` column: "correlated" (new default) or "legacy".
  - "legacy" is the rule used from March 2026 for the round robin (`engine.Best_fit_Chi2`: reduced chi-squared of the kriged grid within 1 of its minimum, i.e. chi-squared within N - 1 for N grid points) and reproduces those results exactly. Before March 2026 (paper era) the range was where grid chi-squared reached min + sqrt(2(N-1)) (`engine.Best_fit_Chi2_old`), with time reported as (row + 1) x dt.
  - Why: the old rules counted 150-300 correlated kriged points as independent data and drew the range by rule of thumb (reduced chi-squared, which Andrae et al. 2010 show is undefined for nonlinear models); the kriging variance was also used as a standard deviation, so it had almost no effect. On synthetic profiles with a known time, the legacy range contained it 99.7% of the time when the model is exact and 62% with smooth misfit (target 68%); the new range 71% and 66%.
  - Effect on the 17 round robin profiles: median 1-sigma width (as a share of the best fit) 11% before March 2026, 34% legacy, 24% new (9-36% by profile); best-fit times move by -14% to +8%. Cost: 0.04-0.07 s per fit against 1.9-8.1 s for the model run; faster than the legacy grid chi-squared.
  - `FitResult` gains `uncertainty`, `fit_x_um`, `fit_fo` (the points fitted) and `noise`; `reduced_chi2` now divides by the number of points fitted.
  - Details, coverage tests and the version comparison: `docs/references/fit-uncertainty.md`, `docs/plans/2026-10-12-fit-uncertainty/`.
  - Method, worked example and validation (2026-10-13): `docs/references/likelihood-method.md`, with figures in `docs/references/figures/likelihood/` and the scripts and data in `docs/plans/2026-10-13-likelihood-validation/`. On synthetic profiles with 29 or more points and smooth misfit, coverage is 53-72% (independent errors 20-66%); the correlation length is recovered once it exceeds the point spacing. Initial-condition errors are not in the single-fit range (a 3 um step error: 21-23%); `forster.scan` covers them (65-74%).

### Fixed

- **Euler-angle tables work with NumPy 2.5 (2026-10-15).** `forster.orientation.vector_direction` called `float()` on a 1x1 matrix, which NumPy 2.5 turned from a deprecation into a TypeError, so `read_runs` failed on any row given as Euler angles (the packaged template included) for a fresh install, which gets NumPy 2.5. It now takes `.item()`; the angles are identical to the last bit on NumPy 2.3 and 2.5.4, and the full test suite passes on both. Found by the Python-version benchmark (`docs/plans/2026-10-15-python-versions.md`).
- **The importance-sampling proposal no longer fails on a nearly singular Laplace covariance (2026-10-15).** scipy's multivariate t refactorised the matrix and could reject one that had already factorised; the density is now computed from the Cholesky factor itself (`sampling._log_t_density`, equal to scipy's to 1e-12). In the validation harness's copy of the method this failed 11 of 70 cases (wide priors).
- CSV tables are read with exact float parsing (`float_precision="round_trip"`). pandas' default parser returned some values 1 ulp off, so a table written by FORSTER did not refit identically.
- Two step positions without `step_fo` (or three or more steps) are now an error naming the problem; the engine used to fail with an IndexError.

- Cross term of the diffusion equation was doubled (`diffusion_step`, found 2026-10-11). The engine solves dC/dt = D d²C/dx² + (dD/dx)(dC/dx). The cross term was built from two central differences, each spanning two grid spacings, so their product needs dividing by 4Δx²; the code divided by 2Δx². The fix changes `/ 2` to `/ 4`. It affects every Fe-Mg, Mn and Ni model where D varies along the profile, and only those (D depends on Fo). Checked against an independent flux-form solver and against DIPRA's published scheme (Girona and Costa 2013, eq. 5), which converge to the same answer as the fixed engine while the old engine does not (`tests/test_physics.py`; details in `docs/plans/2026-10-11-restructure.md`, "Engine fix").
  - Effect on fitted times: idealised single steps came out 1.3% too short for Fo 0.92 to 0.895 and 8-11% too short for Fo 0.82 to 0.885 and 0.67 to 0.745. The 17 round robin fits move by -28% to +43% (most become 13-43% longer). For Towbin and Plank (2026) the melt-vein medians move by 2-5% (1-3 weeks unchanged) and the exterior contact-overgrowth median by +28% (5.9 to 7.5 days).

- **From the 2026-10-14 review** (plan: `docs/plans/2026-10-14-review-fixes.md`):
  - No law range for a rim value that changes in time: like a growth stage, it is fixed in seconds, so the fitted time no longer scales as 1/D (measured: 0.57-0.66 of the D t shift when the rim changes over the first half of the run). Where the law range holds and where it does not: `docs/references/law-uncertainty-limits.md`.
  - A fit with a law covariance whose best fit is at time zero no longer makes `summary_row` raise; the law's error scales the high end (`widen_log10`).
  - Implicit growth stages: a stage starting a rounding error after a time step (for example one ulp) left NaN in the new zone and the solve failed; it now counts as on that step, as the last stage's start already did.
  - Explicit schemes: `fit_profile` raises if the grid is unstable for any Fo the run holds (stage zones and rim values, a fixed `rim_fo`), not only the initial condition's; a lower-Fo zone has a larger D, and the run diverged silently.
  - The coarse time estimate and explicit initial-condition scans size dt on the explicit grid's spacing (they used the implicit grid's: dt up to 5% smaller than needed).
  - `forster fit`: an error of any type in one run is reported with its type and the other runs still reach `summary.csv` (only input errors were caught, so for example an OSError lost the whole summary); KeyError messages print without quotes.
  - Profiles: rows with no distance or Fo are dropped with a warning (the paper workbook passed NaN distances into 6 of its 37 profiles, one of them a measured transect); `Marked_bad` and `Ignore` flags match in any case and with spaces; a template table warns of columns FORSTER does not use (a misspelt optional column used to be ignored silently); a malformed `inflection_c` is a ValueError; `.xls` is refused with the .csv/.xlsx message (openpyxl cannot read it).
  - Docstrings: the implicit scheme's TR-BDF2 is first order in time when D depends on Fo (D is frozen at each stage's start; measured ratio 2.05 when dt halves), second order only for a constant D.
