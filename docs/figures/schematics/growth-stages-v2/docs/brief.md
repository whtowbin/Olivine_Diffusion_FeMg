# Brief: growth stages, version 2 (FORSTER methods paper)

- Mode: revise-style reuse of `../growth-stages` (same look, conventions and code), with
  the zone geometry changed to that of profile 1 of the joint-fit figure. Depth standard,
  route flat, review own. Format: journal, double column, 183 mm wide, 116 mm high.
- Reader: petrologists and volcanologists reading a methods paper; new to FORSTER.
- Style: as growth-stages. One sequential Fo colour scale shared by all the schematics
  (light amber `#F5D27F` = low Fo, `#B9B24F`, olive `#56782B` = high Fo), here over
  Fo 0.80-0.90.

## Takeaway (one sentence)
A profile with several zones can record several growth events; modelled as growth stages,
each zone diffuses only from the time it grew, so inner steps are older and smoother than
outer ones, and FORSTER reports the time since the first and since the last stage.

## Key ideas (the user's criteria first)
1. Same idea as growth-stages: instantaneous growth stages, each new zone then diffusing
   with everything grown before.
2. Two growth stages, so that the zones match joint-fit profile 1 (edge, rim zone,
   mantle, core): stage 0 the core crystal, stage 1 the mantle, stage 2 the rim zone with
   the edge then held at a lower Fo than the zone.
3. Each step visibly relaxes; the inner step is visibly older (smoother) than the outer.
4. Only times after the last stage fit; FORSTER reports both the time since the first
   stage and since the last stage.
5. The comparison with every zone present from time zero, and what FORSTER's near-step
   check says about it on this profile (computed, drawn as it comes out).

## Must not imply
- That the misfit value separates the two histories (the minima are equally low).
- That the near-step check catches the wrong history here: on the drawn profile it does
  not (and only in 2 of 10 noise draws); the residuals, 4.3 times the noise, do.
- Gradual growth (the model is instantaneous steps), or that stage times are fitted (they
  are fixed inputs).
- That the curve is the joint-fit curve: only its zone geometry (step positions, levels,
  length, point spacing) is reused.
- A real crystal, habit or face indices; real-sample numbers.

## Scope
One figure on the growth-stage model with two growth stages and what the fit reports.
