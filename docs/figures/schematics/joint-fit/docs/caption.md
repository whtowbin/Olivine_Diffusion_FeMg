# Caption

**Figure N | Profiles measured on one crystal share one history, so fitting them together gives one time and one edge Fo constrained by both profiles, while each profile keeps its own geometry.**
The joint fit is a planned FORSTER feature, being built now; the profiles and misfit curves here are illustrative, computed for a synthetic crystal (no numeric axes). Colour marks what is shared by the crystal (black and grey) and what each profile has of its own (blue, profile 1; vermillion, profile 2).

**a**, A zoned olivine section (generic outline: the real habit and face indices are not specified) with two roughly orthogonal microprobe traverses, profile 1 at 15° to the fast c axis [001] and profile 2 at 15° to the slow a axis [100]. The circle marks where the traverses cross; that distance along each profile can be given to the fit, and the two models should agree there. The colours are a computed Fo map after diffusion at one time, with Fe-Mg interdiffusion about six times faster along c than along a and Fo held at the edge value along the rim; dashed lines show the zone boundaries of the starting profile. The zones are wider along c than along a: growth differs by direction, so step positions and zone widths are not shared.

**b**, The two Fo profiles, each plotted against distance from its own rim on the same distance scale and the same Fo axis. Shared by the crystal: one time *t*; identical *T*, *P* and *f*O₂; the Fo of each segment (rim zone, mantle, core), matched by its label (grey dashed levels); and, by default, the edge Fo at the rim (black dot; it can be freed for a profile). Own to each profile: its step positions (triangles); its orientation and so its diffusivity, D_profile = D[100]cos²α + D[010]cos²β + D[001]cos²γ (here D₁ ≈ 4.2 D₂, so profile 1's fronts are about twice as wide); an Fo offset on all its segments for microprobe drift between sessions (prior about 0.1 mol%, to be confirmed; drawn on profile 2, enlarged three times; profile 1's is zero here); and an edge position offset of a few µm between the true rim and the first spot (drawn on both). Light lines: the step-shaped starting profile; dark lines: the model at time *t*; dots: synthetic spot analyses with scatter.

**c**, Misfit against time for each profile fitted alone and for the joint fit, whose misfit is the sum of the two (each profile with its own noise model). Fitted: time, edge Fo and the three segment Fo values (shared in the joint fit); step positions and offsets are held at their true values. Bars: 1σ ranges of time (top) and edge Fo (bottom); dotted lines: the true values. Here the joint time range is 20% narrower than profile 1's and 40% narrower than profile 2's; profile 2 alone leaves the edge Fo unbounded on the low side (arrow), because its narrow fronts put few points between the rim and the rim-zone plateau, and the joint edge range is set by the fast profile. Over 12 noise seeds of this example the joint time range was a median 14% narrower than the better single profile's (from 28% narrower to, in two seeds, wider), and the joint edge range a median 9% narrower (wider in three seeds). The gain is modest because the fast profile carries most of the information; quick single-profile fits are run first and their times compared as a check before the joint fit is trusted.
