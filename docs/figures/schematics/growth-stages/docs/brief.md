# Brief: growth stages (FORSTER methods paper)

- Mode: design. Format: journal, double column, 183 mm wide (height about 118 mm).
- Reader: petrologists and volcanologists reading a methods paper; new to FORSTER.
- Style: own choice. Clean flat Nature-style schematic with computed data panels; one
  sequential Fo colour key (amber = low Fo, olive green = high Fo; low Fo is also the
  lighter tone, as Fe-rich olivine is brighter in BSE images) carried through the
  crystal sections and the profile curves.

## Takeaway (one sentence)
A multi-step Fo profile records several growth events, and modelling them as stages
(each zone diffusing only from the time it grew) avoids treating every zone as if it
had existed from time zero.

## Key ideas (the user's criteria first, verbatim)
1. "A crystal grows in instantaneous steps: stage 0 is the starting profile; at each
   later stage's start time the rim moves outward and a new zone with its own Fo
   appears, which then diffuses together with everything grown before."
2. "make clear that inner zones have diffused longer than outer ones"
3. "only times after the last stage fit the measured profile"
4. "FORSTER reports both the time since the first stage and the time since the last
   stage"
5. Treating both zones as present from time zero gives a time that is neither of the two
   (6.7 d here, against 10.1 d and 4.1 d), with an equally good misfit.

## Must not imply
- That the misfit can tell a staged history from a single-stage one (it cannot: equal
  goodness of fit; the history must come from petrography, other elements or zoning).
- That growth is gradual (the model is instantaneous steps) or that stage times are
  fitted (they are fixed inputs).
- A specific crystal habit, face indices or real sample: the crystal is generic and the
  case illustrative.
- That the numbers are a result for a real crystal.

## Scope
One figure on the growth-stage model and what the fit reports. Diffusivity laws, noise
model, Monte Carlo, joint fits: other figures or the text.
